Cheapest of the three item-0026 successors and recommended to run first,
because a negative closes it outright at negligible cost.

Kuperberg Theorem 1.2 bounds the singular-series average over ALL k-subsets
of the window, with no condition on how rank and window grow relative to
each other. The factor it pays is the price of covering the worst subsets.
S1 needs a class-normalized statement over the far thinner family of
realized flank classes at exchange depth. The question is whether that
restriction buys back enough.

Two numbers decide it. If the class-restricted loss is exponential in the
rank alone, the cost exponent collapses and the polylog gate becomes a live
question rather than a settled failure. If the loss is the same, the
hypothesis is closed by arithmetic.

Note before starting: the displayed constant in Theorem 1.2 is Mertens-loose
by a factor per prime, so the honest all-subsets baseline is more expensive
than the previously circulated figure and, below roughly the middle of the
tabulated grid, more expensive than the rank-cost wall it was supposed to
beat. Price against the honest baseline, not the displayed one.

The implied constant in the theorem's asymptotic notation is unexamined.
Whether it matters at exchange depth is part of this item.
