The substantive successor. It is the only registered hypothesis aimed at
the word-grain axis, and word grain is the named absence left by the
literature-grain search: no anchor carries class masses of consecutive-gap
words, while other axes are cleared severally.

The structural claim to be tested: the matrix method fixes residues modulo
a primorial and varies the row index, so a row family is shaped like a
flank class with many members, and the large-sieve density input the method
consumes is a statement about exactly that family. The method's
characteristic defect, that anomalous behaviour appears only along a sparse
sequence of scales, is precisely what the relaxed density axis removes; that
defect is why the method was not worth pricing against the older
all-large-x target.

The decisive objection, which stands until tested and closes the item if it
holds: the method's characteristic output may be a bounded existence
statement rather than a positive proportion of classes. This must be
adjudicated against the method, not against the located theorems, because
the located theorems were surveyed and none states the word-grain form
either way.

Support class of the structural claim is heuristic, and the located
literature contradicts it. The item exists precisely because the two
readings disagree.

Model failure is already established for the method's classical result, so
if the positive-proportion question resolves favourably the separator
requirement is met without further work. Record it as a separate finding
rather than assuming it transfers.
